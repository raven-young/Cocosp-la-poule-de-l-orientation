# Cocosp-la-poule-de-l-orientation | Cocosp — The Orientation Chicken

*A counseling psychology intervention designed to support school and career orientation through tabletop role-playing.*

![Plateau de jeu](PLATEAU_JEU.png)

## Overview

**Cocosp — The Orientation Chicken** is a counseling psychology intervention created to help lower secondary school students better understand their academic and vocational orientation pathways.

The intervention takes the form of a **tabletop role-playing game (TTRPG)** in which participants embody characters, make decisions, and progress through a shared narrative. The objective is not merely entertainment: the game functions as a reflective support tool that helps students explore their choices, resources, obstacles, and the opportunities available within the Swiss educational system.

Rather than presenting orientation only through theoretical explanations, **Cocosp** allows students to *experience* orientation-related situations through symbolic gameplay and collaborative problem-solving.

# Target Audience

The intervention primarily targets:

* Students in **lower secondary education** specifically from Vaud (Switzerland)
* Students enrolled in the **general track**
* Adolescents facing orientation decisions regarding:

	* apprenticeships
	* general education schools
	* academic pathways
	* transition programs
	* bridge solutions

The project specifically addresses the complexity of the Swiss educational system and the challenges students may face when navigating orientation choices at a relatively early age.

# The Problem Addressed

The intervention responds to a central issue:

## Lack of Information

This lack of information concerns two dimensions:

### 1. Information About Educational Opportunities

Students may lack knowledge regarding:

* training programs
* professions
* bridge pathways
* admission requirements
* internships
* alternative routes

### 2. Information About Themselves

Students may also struggle to identify:

* personal interests
* strengths
* values
* competencies
* preferred environments

This uncertainty can make orientation decisions more difficult and may limit students’ perceived possibilities.

---

# Core Concept

In **Cocosp**, players embody chicken characters placed in the “general track.” Their goal is to reach a safe destination called a kingdom of **Formationland**, symbolizing different post-compulsory educational pathways.

Throughout the game, students encounter orientation-related challenges such as:

* improving grades
* preparing application files
* writing CVs and motivation letters
* completing internships
* building Plan A / Plan B strategies
* participating in “class councils”

The game creates a symbolic simulation of orientation processes while maintaining a safe environment where students can experiment without real-world consequences.

# Expected Outcomes

At the end of the intervention, students should be better able to:

* understand educational opportunities
* identify personal strengths and interests
* reflect on orientation decisions
* engage actively in their orientation process

Observable objectives include:

* identifying at least two personal interests or strengths
* naming two possible educational pathways
* explaining access requirements for at least one program

# Role of the Counseling Psychologist

The counselor does not simply facilitate a game.

Their role is to:

* connect gameplay experiences to real-life orientation
* clarify the Swiss educational system
* guide reflective discussions
* support realistic planning
* help students transfer learning into concrete actions

The intervention therefore combines:

* playful engagement
* counseling support
* experiential learning

---

# Debriefing Phase

Debriefing is a central component of the protocol.

After gameplay, students reflect on:

* choices made
* obstacles encountered
* strategies used
* realistic alternatives
* transferable lessons

This stage transforms the game from a simple activity into a genuine orientation-learning experience.

---

# Limitations

**Cocosp** is not intended to replace professional career counseling.

The intervention requires:

* professional facilitation
* accurate educational guidance
* structured debriefing
* contextual adaptation

Without appropriate support, students may oversimplify the educational system or confuse game mechanics with real institutional rules.

---

# Conclusion

**Cocosp** is a counseling psychology intervention using tabletop role-playing to support school and career orientation among students in the general educational track.

The project combines:

* experiential learning
* gamification
* counseling psychology
* vocational guidance
* collaborative reflection

By transforming orientation into an interactive and reflective experience, the intervention aims to make educational pathways more concrete, accessible, and meaningful for adolescents.
